The Problem

Manual outreach does not scale

Most B2B SaaS founders do outreach manually: find a lead, research them, write an email, send it. At 10 leads a day that is 2-3 hours on research alone. At 100 leads it breaks completely.

The deeper problem is that most outreach is untriggered. You are reaching out to founders who may not need you right now. The buying signal gets ignored.

A founder hiring their first SDR or AE is a signal. It means they have decided to scale sales. That is the right moment to offer GTM automation. Not cold, not random. Triggered.

What I Built

Four steps. One pipeline.

01

ICP Filtering

Used Clay's Find People to filter 330M+ profiles down to 5,271 exact ICP matches: B2B SaaS founders, CEO or Co-Founder, 1-50 employees, United States. Capped at 25 for free tier.

Clay Find People ICP Filters
02

Waterfall Email Enrichment

Clay ran a waterfall across 8+ email providers in sequence. If one source fails, the next fires automatically. 10/10 valid work emails found on first batch, including jason@37signals.com, amjad@replit.com, and grant@gamma.app.

LeadMagic Findmail Prospeo Hunter Dropcontact + 3 more
03

AI Personalization Column

Added a Clay AI column with a prompt using First Name, Job Title, and Company Name as live column variables. Clay researched each company before writing. Not template fills. Actual context.

Clay AI Column Dynamic Variables
04

Export to Google Sheets

Downloaded CSV from Clay, imported to Google Sheets. Final sheet has full name, company, job title, validated work email, and AI-written email copy. Ready for Gmail or n8n outreach.

The Hard Part

Variables, credits, and getting AI to research before writing

The Clay AI column needed dynamic column references, not plain text placeholders. Typing {{First Name}} does not work. You type / and select from a dropdown. Clay pulls live column data, not static strings. That distinction matters at scale.

Free tier management was a real constraint. 100 credits total. Waterfall enrichment costs 3-5 credits per row. That forced the right instinct: test on 10 rows before running 25. Never waste credits on a broken prompt.

// Clay AI column prompt structure
Write a cold email from Vinayak (GTM engineer)
to [/First Name] at [/Company Name].

Output ONLY the email. No subject line.

Hi [/First Name],

[1 sentence about what they do at Company Name]
[1 sentence: I build trigger-based outbound systems]
[1 specific question about their outbound process]

Vinayak

The AI did not just fill slots. It researched each company before writing. For Thrive Global it identified their enterprise wellness positioning. For 37signals it referenced their product philosophy. That is the difference between personalization and variable injection.

What I Learned

Clay is a logic layer, not just a data tool

The waterfall is the product

No single email provider has 100% coverage. The waterfall chains 8+ sources and stops when one succeeds. 10/10 on the first batch. That is not luck, that is system design.

Free tier constraints sharpen thinking

100 credits forces you to think about cost per outcome. Test 10 rows, validate quality, then scale. That is the right mental model for any production system regardless of budget.

AI at the column level changes what is possible in a day

In n8n I needed a full workflow to get AI into the loop. In Clay it is one column with a prompt. The speed difference is real and changes how fast you can ship.

The GTM skill is filter design, not button clicking

5,271 results from 330M+ profiles in under 5 minutes is the easy part. Knowing which filters produce the right ICP is where the judgment lives. The tool is fast. The thinking is the work.
